# The Long Watch

A multiplayer game that grows one small piece per run: an island at night and the people who keep its
lanterns. Right now (milestone 15) players pick one of six lantern rooms, walk the seeded island with its seven landmarks
(old beacons, a ruined lighthouse, a jetty, a well) and a minimap, in light that changes through an eight-hour night,
see everyone else in the same room move (footsteps are available and off by default), and talk with 5 gestures and 16 preset phrases (ids only, never typed text), with arrows
at the screen edge pointing to players who are off screen. They gather driftwood, reeds and oil berries, give them (and the wicks and lanterns they craft) to each other, and craft wicks and amber, jade or azure lanterns whose light reaches further for everyone nearby. Mist and rain drift over the island and the sky strip darkens when shades come (all drawn by each browser from the seed and the clock). Figures walk with a stepping stride and a swinging lantern, and bow, wave and cheer with their own poses (each browser draws them alone; still with reduced motion). The island is alive: glinting water, shore foam, swaying trees, fireflies round lanterns and a moon that crosses the sky strip as the night goes on (all of it holds still with reduced motion). Each day (UTC) the keeper writes three small notes from the seed and the date (gather some of one thing, make wicks or amber lanterns, burn fuel in one named beacon) that everyone in a lantern works on together; a note needs at least two players. At night, slow hooded shades drift in off the sea, circle a landmark and snuff the lantern of anyone they reach for 14 seconds; they hurt no one, and light drives them off, but a shade is only driven off when at least two players have put light on it, and a friend's light holds it back and relights you. When all four old beacons in a lantern burn at once, the ruined lighthouse lights for everyone there until the lantern empties, and shades keep out of its beam (worked out by each browser from the beacons, nothing extra is sent; the shades' places rely on players' device clocks agreeing). Helping others (relighting a friend, driving off a shade together, a gift that arrives, lighting a beacon or finishing a keeper's note together) earns points toward a lamplighter rank, shown as a title and gold marks on your lantern; it is decoration only. Peer to peer over WebRTC (Trystero, signalling through public Nostr relays, or public BitTorrent trackers if those cannot be reached, with the connected path shown on the page; the owner has played it online on an earlier version; this milestone is
untested), no
server, no accounts, no text between players.
