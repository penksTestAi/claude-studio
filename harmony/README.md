# Harmony of the Worlds

An instrument built on Johannes Kepler's *Harmonices Mundi* (1619). One HTML file, Web Audio, no build.

- **Open locally:** double-click `index.html`, then press **Play sound** (browsers only start audio after a click).
- **Online (private until you share it):** https://claude.ai/artifact/7wx2ABiDWq2PuZNeLv3X7V

## Kepler's six planets
Mercury to Saturn on their real elliptical orbits (J2000 mean elements, solved with Kepler's equation),
starting at today's positions. Each planet hums a tone whose pitch follows its angular speed seen from the
Sun, so it slides up at perihelion and down at aphelion. The panel compares Kepler's intervals with the
ratio the modern orbits give, `((1+e)/(1-e))²`:

| Planet  | Kepler 1619          | Orbit today |
|---------|----------------------|-------------|
| Saturn  | 5:4 major third 1.250 | 1.241 |
| Jupiter | 6:5 minor third 1.200 | 1.214 |
| Mars    | 3:2 fifth 1.500       | 1.455 |
| Earth   | 16:15 semitone 1.067  | 1.069 |
| Venus   | 25:24 diesis 1.042    | 1.028 |
| Mercury | 12:5 octave + minor third 2.400 | 2.303 |

The shaded wedges are swept in equal times (1/12 of the selected planet's year) and have equal areas:
Kepler's second law, drawn to true scale.

## Resonant chains
TRAPPIST-1, Jupiter's Galilean moons, Kepler-223, and Neptune–Pluto. Orbit radii follow Kepler's third law,
pitch is proportional to orbital frequency, and each body plucks as it crosses the top of the dial, so a 3:2
resonance is heard as a perfect fifth and 2:1 as an octave.
