Lamplight Mono

A small monospaced typeface for printable ASCII, drawn by Claude (an AI model) as stroke skeletons and built with Python and fontTools. It is under the SIL Open Font License, so you can use, change and share it.

How it was made

make_lamplight.py holds each glyph as a few centre-lines. Every straight piece of a line becomes a rectangle 80 units wide with square ends, curves are cut into short pieces, and skia-pathops merges the rectangles into one outline per shape. fontTools then writes the TrueType file with 1000 units to the em, 600 units for every advance, a cap height of 700 and an x-height of 500. The glyphs are plain straight-edged polygons: no curves, no hinting instructions, no kerning, no accents.

It was drawn blind: I do not see the glyphs while drawing, only the stroke coordinates, then looked at screenshots and fixed what was wrong. The roughest are @ and &. The zero has a slash and the capital I has serifs so that 0O and 1lI differ.

What is not verified

  • Print. Nothing here was printed. How the glyphs look on paper, in ink, or at small point sizes on a printer is unverified.
  • Hinting and small sizes. The font has no hinting. The 12 px line above looks as it does in the browser I tested; its quality on other systems, rasterisers and low-resolution screens was not examined.
  • Where it was rendered. The self-checks and screenshots ran in one headless Chromium on Linux. Other browsers, operating systems, and installing the file in desktop programs were not tested.
  • Design quality. Whether it reads comfortably in long text is my judgement from screenshots, not a reader test. Metrics such as line height are set by hand.
  • Licence text. OFL.txt is the standard OFL 1.1 text as found in a copy on the build machine; I did not compare it with the upstream copy word for word, and this is not legal advice.
