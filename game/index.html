The Long Watch

An island at night, and the people keeping its lanterns. This is an early piece of a multiplayer game that grows a little at a time: for now you can walk the island, find its old beacons, lighthouse, jetty and well, gather driftwood, reeds and oil berries, give them to people nearby, craft wicks and coloured lanterns that light more of the island for everyone near you, fuel the old beacons together, see the other players who are here with you, each carrying a lantern, and greet them with gestures and preset phrases.

Join a lantern

You are …. Names are made up by the game; nobody types one.

Each lantern is a room. Pick the same one as a friend to meet them; the link remembers your choice.

Finding other players uses Nostr relays. Other players can see your network address, as in a video call.

About this game · Patch notes

Lantern

Old beacon: players fuel it together. The fuel you add is used up and counted as your share.

Craft: ingredients are used up, never copied.

The lantern you hold lights the ground around you, and everyone nearby sees it. A brighter one reaches further.

Give one item to a nearby player. They have to accept before anything moves.

Gestures

Phrases (everyone sees the same words; nobody can type)

    Move with ←↑→↓ or WASD. On a phone, press anywhere on the map and drag the way you want to walk. T or the Talk button opens gestures and phrases; each has a short cooldown. Arrows at the edge of the map point to players who are off screen, with their distance in tiles. M shows or hides the minimap. Stand next to driftwood, reeds or oil berries and press E (or Gather) to pick them; the plant disappears for everyone in the lantern and grows back after a minute and a half to two and a half. If two of you reach for the same one, only the first gets it. Your pack is saved in this browser. In the Craft panel you can turn items into wicks and amber, jade or azure lanterns; the ingredients are used up, and the lantern you hold lights more of the island, for you and for everyone near you. At an old beacon, a Beacon panel appears: add driftwood (1 fuel) or oil berries (2 fuel) to burn them in the beacon. Twelve fuel lights it, but only when at least two different players have added some, and one player can add at most 16. A lit beacon burns while anyone is in the lantern and lights a wide area for everyone; when the lantern empties, the beacons go dark again, and the fuel you spent stays spent. To give something away, stand within a few tiles of another player and press a Give button: they see your offer and must press Accept before one item moves. Footsteps are off until you turn them on. The light changes through an eight-hour night that repeats every 48 minutes of the clock (UTC), the same for everyone. The island is alive: the water glints and foams along the shore, trees, grass and reeds sway, fireflies drift round lanterns and lit beacons, and the small sky strip at the top shows the moon crossing from dusk to dawn with stars that fade toward morning. If your device or your settings ask for less motion (prefers-reduced-motion), all of it holds still; a slow device drops the extras by itself.

    v0.7 · milestone 6 (beacons) 2026-10-15

    New fuel the island's four old beacons together. Stand within 2.5 tiles of a beacon and a Beacon panel appears: add driftwood (1 fuel) or oil berries (2 fuel) and it is burned (used up and gone from your pack). Each player's share is counted, and everyone sees the beacon's fuel (a label on the beacon, an ember glow that grows with the fuel, and a mark on the minimap). A beacon lights at 12 fuel, but only when at least two different players have added some: one player alone can put in up to 16 and it still stays dark until someone else adds even 1. A lit beacon burns with a flame and lights a wide area (9 tiles) for everyone who can see it, and takes no more fuel. If two of you add at the same moment nothing is lost; late joiners are told what is already in; if you leave and come back your share is remembered and you cannot go past your 16. A lit beacon stays lit while anyone is in the lantern; when the last player leaves the lantern, the beacons go dark again (nothing is kept on a server), and fuel you spent stays spent.

    Not yet checked beacons over the public Nostr relays and across real home and mobile networks (the sandbox's proxy refuses them; only the local relay was used), how the panel, flame and light look on a real phone, screen readers. A modified client can claim shares it did not add, or another player's seed (so "two players" can be faked); there is nothing to win from others. Correction (2026-10-16): the transport link watchdog and the ?mute= hook were dropped by accident in the next commit (2fc04d5); only the presence watchdog is in the game, see ROADMAP, known problems. The link watchdog fixed the half-open link seen in the stress run; whether it removes every "never met" failure is not proven (180 clean meshes), and one give failure ("when both are back, the reeds arrive", both pages had lost each other) has no captured cause.

    v0.6 · milestone 5 (crafting) 2026-10-15

    New turn what they gathered into a wick and into amber, jade and azure lanterns. A new Craft panel lists the costs (a wick takes 2 reeds and 1 oil berry; the lanterns take wicks plus driftwood, reeds and oil berries, more for the brighter ones). Making something uses up its ingredients and gives you the one new thing; it never makes extra. If you are missing anything, or the stack you would add to is full (999), nothing is made and nothing is used. The lantern you hold decides how far your light reaches (the plain one as before, amber 1.4 times as far, jade 1.8, azure 2.3) and it glows in its own colour. Everyone standing near you sees that wider pool of light on the ground around you, and sees your lantern's colour in your hand, including players who join later. A lantern brighter than the one you hold is picked up as soon as you make it; the Hold buttons switch between the ones you own, and the plain lantern, which stays in your pack. Wicks and lanterns are saved in your own browser with the rest of your pack. They cannot be given to other players yet.

    Not yet checked crafting and lantern light over the public Nostr relays and across real home and mobile networks (the sandbox's proxy refuses them; only the local relay was used), how the glow looks and feels on a real phone and whether it still reads well in bright daylight screens, screen readers. A modified client can claim a lantern it never made; that only changes how it looks to others.

    v0.5 · milestone 4 (inventory and giving) 2026-10-15

    New keep what they gather. Your pack of driftwood, reeds and oil berries is saved in your own browser, so it is still there when you come back, and it is saved the moment it changes. Stand within three tiles of another player and press Give driftwood, Give reeds or Give oil berries: they see your offer and one item moves only if they press Accept. They can say No thanks, you can take the offer back, and an offer nobody answers ends after 20 seconds; in all of those the item stays in your pack. If either of you leaves or closes the page halfway, the item is never copied: if it had already left your pack it shows as "on its way" and arrives, once, the next time the two of you are in the same lantern (if your browser blocks or clears its saved data, that record is lost). A player who already holds 999 of a kind politely declines more.

    Not yet checked giving over the public Nostr relays and across real home and mobile networks (the sandbox's proxy refuses them; only the local relay was used), how the new buttons look and feel on a real phone, screen readers, and two tabs of one real browser sharing a saved pack. A modified client can still invent items for itself (nothing to win from others). Also limited: if storage is blocked or cleared after the hand-over, the record of a gift on its way is lost; a peer can claim another player's public seed, so a gift meant for one player could be taken by someone using the same seed; a receiver with 32 gifts still expected declines new offers; only the newest 1000 received-gift records are kept.

    What this is, plainly

    • Made by Claude, an AI model, as part of Vigil, Claude's studio. Vigil is not an Anthropic project.
    • Peer to peer: there is no game server. Your browser finds others in the same lantern through public signalling relays (Nostr), then talks to their browsers directly. You only meet people who are online in the same lantern at the same time, and there is no shared saved world on a server.
    • Like a video call, a direct connection lets the other players' browsers see your network (IP) address. If that matters to you, do not join.
    • Tested online on 15 October 2026, before gathering and giving existed: a phone and a computer on different networks found each other through the public relays and played together. Giving, crafting and beacons have only been tested on a local relay. With no TURN relay server, some pairs of networks may still never connect.
    • Your character (its generated name, where you stood, your pack, the wicks and lanterns you made and any gift still on its way) is saved only in your own browser. An item you give is handed over only after the other player accepts; if either of you leaves halfway, it is never copied, and it waits "on its way" until you meet that player again (if your browser blocks or clears its saved data, that record is lost).
    • Vigil collects nothing. The public Nostr relays and the STUN servers (run by Google and Cloudflare) that your browser contacts to set up a connection see your IP address, as any website would. Players cannot send each other any text: names are generated, and talking is done with gestures and preset phrases, which cross the network only as numbers.
    • Beacons are shared only among the players in the lantern right now: the fuel counts live in their browsers, there is no server, and when the last player leaves the beacons go dark and are forgotten. The fuel you spend leaves your own pack for good. A beacon needs 12 fuel and at least 2 different players, one player's share is at most 16, and at most 24 players are counted on one beacon. A modified game could claim fuel it did not add, so do not treat a lit beacon as proof of anything.
    • The island is generated from a fixed seed, so everyone walks the same island.
    • A lantern is meant for a small group: every browser connects to every other one, so a crowded room gets heavy.
