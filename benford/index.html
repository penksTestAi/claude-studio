Why Real Numbers Start With 1

Real data often starts with 1 more than any other digit, but only if it spans orders of magnitude, and small samples cannot show it.

Made by Claude, an AI model, for Vigil. Every count and test on this page is computed by the page from big-integer sequences or from numbers other Vigil pieces already ship. No outside dataset is used. Self-checks are listed at the bottom; what is not checked is listed after them.

The idea: a ruler with no even spacing

Benford's law says the first digit d turns up with share log10(1 + 1/d). Lay the numbers 1 to 10 on a logarithmic ruler and each digit owns the stretch from log10d to log10(d+1). If a quantity wanders over many powers of ten, so that where it lands on one decade of this ruler is close to even, each digit gets a share equal to its stretch. Sequences computed exactly

These are computed with exact big integers (no rounding), then the first digit of each term is counted. Pick a sequence and how many terms to use. Red rings are the Benford shares; bars are what the terms did.

Numbers the studio already ships

These lists were read from other Vigil pieces by make_data.py (the sources are named on each card). Each card counts first digits of the values, taking absolute values and leaving out zeros. The first group is the one I expected to fit; the second group is the one I expected not to. Each card shows how many powers of ten its values span, because a list can be bounded or assigned and still span two. The verdicts are computed, so they may disagree with my expectation, and with many tests at the 0.05 level a few rejections are expected by chance alone.

Sample size changes the verdict

The test used is chi-square against the Benford shares with 8 degrees of freedom, rejecting when p is below 0.05, together with the mean absolute deviation (MAD) of the nine shares. Two things to see: a small sample often cannot reject anything, and a big enough sample rejects even a small departure.

How often the test rejects, over repeated samples

For each sample size the page draws fresh samples, and counts how many the chi-square test rejects at 0.05. "True Benford" draws come from the exact shares, so any rejection there is a false alarm. "Nudged" draws take percentage points from digit 1 and give them to digit 2: a designed, small departure (the note under the table gives its MAD).

Self-checks

    What is not verified

    • The MAD bands (close, acceptable, marginal, nonconforming) are conventions I recalled from the forensic-accounting literature. I did not check them against a source, so they are labelled as a convention and not counted as a check.
    • That powers of 2 and Fibonacci numbers follow the law because their logarithms step by an irrational amount is a known result I am quoting, not one this page proves. The page only measures the first terms.
    • Whether factorials converge to the law is only observed here. A small chi-square is not proof.
    • A not-rejected verdict says the test did not detect a departure at that sample size. It does not say the data are genuine, and a rejection does not say they are faked.
    • The studio lists are small, were built for other purposes, and are not random samples of anything. Small counts give a rough chi-square p; each card says when.
    • No outside dataset was fetched. A web search of other people's Benford results was not used, so nothing here rests on search summaries.
